Indian Banking Sector Deep Dive: Deposit Competition, NIM Normalization & RoA Decomposition

Analyzing balance sheet health, Credit-to-Deposit (C/D) friction, and Du Pont Return-on-Assets decomposition across India's top private and public lenders.

1. The Deposit Growth Squeeze

While systemic loan growth has compounded at ~14-16%, deposit mobilization has lagged at ~11-12%. As household savings increasingly shift toward equity mutual funds and SIPs, banks face higher costs of term deposits to fund asset expansion.

2. Post-HDFC Merger Rebalancing

The mega-merger of HDFC Ltd into HDFC Bank introduced elevated C/D ratio pressure (~104% peak). HDFC Bank is intentionally moderating credit growth below systemic levels to replace high-cost wholesale borrowings with granular retail deposits.

3. Multi-Decade Clean Asset Quality

Net NPAs across leading private lenders (ICICI, HDFC, Axis, Kotak) remain below 0.5%, with credit costs running at cyclical troughs (~40-60 bps). Robust corporate balance sheet health and disciplined underwriting have preserved profitability.

Equity Research Analytical Takeaways

1. ICICI Bank's Operational Dominance: ICICI Bank has established best-in-class risk-adjusted profitability, achieving an industry-leading 2.35% RoA supported by superior digital cross-selling, low branch operating overhead, and disciplined provisioning. Its core operating profit growth provides the highest valuation safety cushion among peers.

2. HDFC Bank's Valuation Rerating Catalyst: HDFC Bank currently trades at a historical discount to its 10-year mean P/ABV (~2.6x vs ~3.8x peak). The key catalyst for valuation rerating will be sequential reduction in its Credit-to-Deposit ratio below 88% and sustained granular retail deposit accretion without margin erosion.

3. PSU Bank Convergence (SBI): SBI achieves a competitive RoE of ~16.8% primarily through financial leverage (~15.2x assets-to-equity) rather than high RoA (1.04%). While its asset quality has substantially hardened, public sector wage revisions and lower fee income generation keep its RoA structurally lower than tier-1 private peers.
